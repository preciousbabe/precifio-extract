📖 What Is Reconciliation?

Reconciliation is the process of comparing two sets of records to find which ones belong together. Precifio Reconcile does this automatically using a multi-signal scoring engine.

Think of it as pairing socks from two laundry baskets. You don't need a brand list. You look at color, size, and pattern. Precifio does the same with your documents — it reads the data inside them and figures out which invoice matches which payment, which PO matches which delivery note, or which payroll record matches which bank debit.

Side A could be invoices. Side B could be bank statements, purchase orders, delivery notes, or anything else. The engine doesn't care what you call the documents. It cares about the signals inside them: amounts, dates, reference numbers, vendor names, and more.

💡 Key Insight: Precifio is document-agnostic. You can reconcile invoices against bank lines, purchase orders against vendor invoices, payroll against bank debits, or even medical records against lab results. If both sides have structured data, Precifio can match them.

🧠 How the Matching Engine Works

Precifio doesn't do simple lookup tables. It uses a multi-signal weighted scoring engine that evaluates every possible pair and assigns a confidence score from 0 to 100.

The Four Signals

SignalWeightWhat It Checks
Amount 35% Do the numbers match exactly or within tolerance? Supports currency checks.
Reference 25% Do invoice numbers, PO numbers, check numbers, or transaction IDs align? Normalizes punctuation.
Date 25% Are the dates within proximity? Validates sequence (e.g., invoice before payment).
Vendor / Party 15% Fuzzy text match on vendor names, payees, or customer names.

How Scoring Works

Each signal gets a sub-score (0.0 to 1.0) multiplied by its weight. The sum is the Total Score. Separately, the engine calculates a Gate Score — the score across only the "hard gate" fields (amount, reference, currency). A match must pass its gates to be trusted, even if soft fields like description score poorly.

Example: Why a Perfect Match Might Score 71%

Amount
100% × 0.35 = 35pts
Reference
100% × 0.25 = 25pts
Date
80% × 0.25 = 20pts
Vendor
100% × 0.15 = 15pts
Total
95% — Matched

If the description field were also compared and scored 20%, the total might drop to ~71% — but the match is still Exact because the hard gates are perfect.

Matching Phases (In Order)

  1. Exact Reference Lock: Pairs where reference numbers match exactly and amounts are within tolerance are locked in immediately. No other match can "steal" these documents.
  2. Sum Matching (Partial Payments): The engine looks for multiple Side B items (payments, credits, adjustments) that sum to one Side A item (invoice).
  3. Sum Matching (Splits / Deposits): The engine looks for multiple Side A items (invoices) that sum to one Side B item (a batch deposit or consolidated payment).
  4. 1:1 Fuzzy Matching: Remaining documents are scored pairwise. High-confidence pairs (90%+) auto-match. Medium-confidence (60–89%) go to Review.
  5. Unmatched Analysis: Documents with no suitable match get a detailed report explaining why, including the top 3 closest candidates and why they failed.
💡 Why This Order Matters: By locking exact references first and running sum matching second, the engine prevents a greedy 1:1 match from "stealing" a document that should be part of a split or partial payment.

📊 Understanding Match Types

After running reconciliation, results are grouped into tabs based on their classification. Here's what each one means:

TypeScoreWhat It MeansAction Needed
Exact 90–100% All hard gates (amount, reference, currency) align perfectly. High confidence. None. Auto-approved.
Matched 75–89% Most fields align well. Likely correct but worth a quick glance. Quick review recommended.
Partial Sum 80–89% Multiple Side B items (e.g., payments + credit notes) add up to one Side A item (e.g., an invoice). Verify each leg of the sum. Check that credits are correctly applied.
Split 75–84% Multiple Side A items (e.g., invoices) add up to one Side B item (e.g., a batch deposit). Verify allocation per invoice. Check for unallocated balances.
Review 60–74% Some fields match but others are off — typically amount variance or date anomaly while reference checks out. Human review required. Open the Investigative Report to see why.
Unmatched — No suitable match found. Either missing from the other side, or key fields don't align. Check the Analysis tab for candidate explanations. May need to upload missing documents.

Hard Gates vs. Soft Signals

Not all fields are treated equally. Hard gates must pass for a match to be trusted. Soft signals influence the score but don't block a match on their own.

🔒 Hard Gates

Amount, Reference, Currency

If these fail, the match is either rejected entirely or sent to Review. A wrong reference almost always means the wrong transaction.

📡 Soft Signals

Date, Vendor, Description

These influence the total score and trigger warnings (e.g., "date anomaly"), but a perfect amount + reference can still auto-match even if the description differs.

⚠️ Important: For sum matching to work, you must enable it in the Match Rules screen and specify which field contains the amount on each side. Without this, the engine only does 1:1 matching and will miss partial payments, credits, and splits entirely.

How the Engine Validates Splits

When the engine proposes a split or partial sum, it doesn't just check the math. It validates:

  1. Amount Math: Do the items sum to the target within tolerance?
  2. Vendor Consistency: Do all items in the subset belong to the same vendor (fuzzy match ≥ 80%)?
  3. Reference Links: Does at least one item contain a reference to the target document? (Relaxed for deposits, which rarely carry individual invoice numbers.)
  4. Transaction Type Awareness: Credit notes and adjustments are treated as negative contributors, not as standalone payments.
💡 Pro Tip: If your payment memos contain text like "Payment for INV-001 and INV-002", Precifio automatically extracts those embedded invoice numbers during upload and uses them to validate splits — even if there's no dedicated "reference" column.

1

Prepare Your Data (Two Ways)

Option A — From Extraction: Upload and extract your primary documents (Side A) using Precifio Extract. PDFs, images, scans — anything works. Then click 🔄 Reconcile from the Export dropdown.

Option B — Direct Upload: Click 🔄 Reconcile Documents on your dashboard. Create a workspace, then upload CSV, Excel, or JSON files directly to Side A and Side B. No extraction needed.

2

Grant Consent (First Time Only)

Reconciliation requires storing your structured extracted data so we can compare documents. Original files are never kept — only text and numbers. You'll see a consent screen explaining this on your first run.

3

Create or Choose a Workspace

A workspace is a folder for one reconciliation job. Create one per month, project, or client. Example names: "August 2026 Bank Rec" or "Q3 Vendor Reconciliation".

4

Upload Side B (Comparison Data)

Upload your comparison dataset. This can be a CSV/Excel/JSON export from your bank, accounting software, or ERP. Each row becomes a document. Column headers become field names automatically.

5

Review Match Rules (Critical Step)

Click ⚙ Match Rules before running. Precifio auto-detects field mappings, but you should verify:

  • Are the correct amount fields mapped?
  • Is the date tolerance appropriate for your workflow?
  • Is Sum Matching enabled if you expect partial payments or deposits?
6

Run Reconciliation

Click ▶ Run Reconciliation. The engine compares every document on Side A against every document on Side B. For datasets under 500 documents per side, this completes in seconds. Larger datasets are queued for background processing.

7

Review the Four Tabs

Results are organized into Matched, Partial, Review, and Unmatched. Click any result to open its Investigative Report — a detailed breakdown of why it matched, why it didn't, and what actions to take.

8

Export or Iterate

Download a PDF or Excel report of your entire reconciliation. If results look wrong, adjust your Match Rules (tolerances, weights, sum matching) and re-run. There's no limit on re-runs.

⚙️ Deep Dive: Match Rules

Match rules are the DNA of your reconciliation. They tell Precifio which fields to compare, how strictly to compare them, and what to do when things don't align perfectly.

Comparison Strategies

StrategyBest ForHow It WorksTolerance
Exact with Tolerance Amounts, totals, quantities Parses currency symbols, commas, decimals. Compares numeric values. Absolute (e.g., ±₦0.01) + Percentage (e.g., ±1%)
Date Proximity Invoice dates, payment dates, due dates Parses multiple date formats. Calculates days between dates. Days (e.g., ±7 days)
Normalized Exact Reference numbers, invoice #, PO #, check # Strips punctuation, spaces, and case. "INV-001" = "inv001". None (exact) or fuzzy fallback at 90% similarity
Exact Currencies, statuses, categories Case-insensitive string comparison. "NGN" = "ngn". None
Fuzzy Text Vendor names, descriptions, memos Levenshtein distance similarity. Handles typos and abbreviations. Similarity threshold (e.g., 75%)

Weights: How Much Each Field Matters

Weights are decimals from 0 to 1. They determine how much each field contributes to the final score. The default weights are:

  • Amount: 0.35 (highest — the most important signal)
  • Reference: 0.25
  • Date: 0.25
  • Vendor / Text: 0.15

You can adjust these in the Match Rules screen. For example, if you're reconciling purchase orders against delivery notes where PO numbers are unreliable, reduce the reference weight and increase the vendor weight.

Gates: The Hard Stops

A gate is a field that must pass for a match to be considered valid. If a gate fails, the match is either rejected or downgraded to Review.

🔒 Gate = ON

Amount, Reference, Currency

If amount is off by 20%, the match is unmatched regardless of how good the reference is. This prevents false positives.

📡 Gate = OFF

Date, Vendor, Description

If the date is 10 days late, the match score drops but the match can still proceed if amount + reference are perfect.

Tolerances: Allowing for Real-World Variance

Not all matches are perfect. Tolerances let you define "close enough":

  • Amount Tolerance: Set an absolute value (e.g., ±₦1.00) and a percentage (e.g., ±1%). A payment of ₦4,950 against an invoice of ₦5,000 passes if your tolerance is ±₦50 or ±1%.
  • Date Tolerance: Set in days. A payment dated August 5 against an invoice dated August 1 passes if tolerance is ≥ 4 days.
💡 Pro Tip: For bank reconciliation, set date tolerance to 3–5 days (payments clear quickly). For international wire transfers, set it to 7–14 days. For accrual-based accounting, you might want date as a hard gate to catch period mismatches.

Sum Matching Configuration

To enable partial payments, credits, and split deposits:

  1. Open ⚙ Match Rules
  2. Check "Enable Sum Matching"
  3. Enter the Side A Amount Field (e.g., total)
  4. Enter the Side B Amount Field (e.g., amount)
  5. Set Tolerance % (start with 0, increase only if you have rounding issues)

Without sum matching enabled, the engine will never detect that two payments of ₦3,000 and ₦250 equal one invoice of ₦3,250. It will instead try to match each payment individually and likely fail.

🔗 Field Aliases: Cross-Format Matching

Different systems use different field names. Your accounting software exports "Total Due" while your bank exports "Amount". Your PO system uses "PO Number" while your vendor uses "Order Ref". Field aliases bridge these gaps.

How Aliases Work

You define a canonical name (the standard name Precifio recognizes) and a list of aliases (the variations your files might use). During reconciliation, all aliases are normalized to the canonical name before matching.

🔍 Investigative Reports & Anomaly Detection

Every match — and every unmatched document — gets a detailed investigative report. This isn't just a score. It's a forensic breakdown of why the engine made its decision, what signals passed, what failed, and what you should do next.

What's Inside an Investigative Report

📊 Scoring Breakdown

Total score, gate score, and whether all hard gates were present. Shows exactly how each field contributed to the final number.

🚩 Gate Failures

A list of which hard gates failed and why: amount_mismatch, reference_mismatch, currency_mismatch, etc.

⚠️ Warnings

Soft-signal issues that don't block the match but deserve attention: date_sequence_violation, date_variance, etc.

📝 Investigative Notes

Human-readable narratives explaining the anomaly, severity levels (low / medium / high / critical), and suggested actions.

Example: Amount Variance Report

Invoice vs Payment with a Difference

Side A — Invoice
Total: ₦5,000.00
vs
Side B — Payment
Amount: ₦4,950.00

Investigative Note: "The payment is ₦50.00 below the invoice amount (1% of base). This could indicate a partial payment, early payment discount, credit note, or deduction."

Suggested Actions:

  • Check payment terms for early payment discounts
  • Review credit notes or adjustments
  • Verify if retention or withholding applies
  • Confirm partial payment agreement

Example: Unmatched Document Analysis

When a document has no match, the report doesn't just say "unmatched." It shows:

  • The document's own amount, reference, and date
  • The top 3 closest candidates from the other side
  • Why each candidate failed (gate failures, score too low, already matched to another document)
  • Specific actions: "Verify if payment is pending," "Check for duplicate invoice," "Review if amount was adjusted"

AI-Enhanced Narratives

If you have OpenAI integration enabled, the engine generates a concise 2–3 sentence executive summary of each anomaly. This appears in the Analysis tab and gives you the "so what" without reading every field-level detail.

✅ Pro Tips for Perfect Results

  1. Use consistent date formats. ISO format (YYYY-MM-DD) works best, but most common formats are auto-parsed.
  2. Include reference numbers. Invoice numbers, PO numbers, check numbers, and transaction IDs are the strongest matching signals. Without them, the engine relies heavily on amount + date, which is less reliable.
  3. Include a "type" column in your CSV. Values like payment, credit_note, adjustment, or deposit help the engine apply the correct math and generate better investigative reports.
  4. Clean your CSV before upload. Remove empty rows, header repeats, and summary totals that aren't actual line items. Summary rows create false matches.
  5. Name your fields clearly. "amount", "total", "payment" are auto-recognized as numeric. "ref", "reference", "invoice_number" are recognized as identifiers.
  6. Start with a small batch. Test with 10–20 documents to verify your match rules before running on hundreds.
  7. Enable Sum Matching for real-world accounting. If you only do 1:1 matching, you'll miss partial payments, credit notes, and batch deposits.
  8. Set date tolerance based on your payment method. Local bank transfers: 3 days. International wires: 7–14 days. Cash: same day.
  9. Review the "Review" tab carefully. These are the matches the engine is unsure about. A 2-minute human check prevents errors.
  10. Use workspaces for time periods. Create one workspace per month or per project. Don't dump everything into one giant workspace — it slows down matching and creates cross-period false positives.
  11. Set up Field Aliases early. Do it once in Settings and every future reconciliation benefits from better auto-detection.
  12. Re-run after adjusting rules. There's no limit on re-runs. If the first result looks wrong, tweak tolerances or weights and try again.

❓ Troubleshooting

"No matches found"

  • Confirm both Side A and Side B have data. Check the Side A / Side B tabs.
  • Review your Match Rules. Are the correct amount fields mapped? Is Sum Matching enabled if needed?
  • Check if your amount fields are actually numeric. Text like "$1,000" is handled, but "One thousand" is not.
  • Verify your date formats are parseable. Try YYYY-MM-DD if unsure.

"Too many unmatched items"

  • Increase the date tolerance in Match Rules (e.g., from 3 days to 7 or 14 days).
  • Increase the amount tolerance percentage slightly (e.g., from 0% to 1%).
  • Enable or increase fuzzy matching weights for vendor names.
  • Check if your CSV has summary rows or totals that shouldn't be treated as individual documents. Remove them.
  • Verify your Field Aliases are set up. If the engine can't find "amount" because your column is called "amt_paid", it won't match.

"Wrong documents are matching"

  • Add more match rules with higher weights. Reference numbers are the strongest signal.
  • Reduce the amount tolerance if amounts are matching incorrectly across different vendors.
  • Make the vendor field a hard gate if cross-vendor matches are happening.
  • Check the "Review" tab — these are the ambiguous matches that need your attention.

"Split matches have no reference proof"

  • Ensure your payment memos or description fields contain invoice numbers. The engine extracts embedded references automatically.
  • If references are in a separate column, make sure it's mapped as a reference-type rule.
  • For batch deposits with no individual references, the engine will still match by vendor + amount math, but will flag it as "batch deposit" in the report.

"Credit notes show as unmatched"

  • Make sure Sum Matching is enabled in Match Rules.
  • Ensure your credit notes have a type column with value credit_note or similar.
  • Verify the credit note and the corresponding payment share the same vendor.
  • Check that the credit note amount is negative (e.g., -2000.00) in your CSV.

"I can't delete my data"

  • Open any workspace and click ⚙ Settings in the top-right.
  • Scroll to Data Management and click Delete All Data.
  • Type DELETE to confirm. This is permanent and instant.

🔒 Privacy & Data Security

🔐 Zero Retention by Default

When you use Precifio Extract without Reconciliation, your documents and extracted data are purged immediately after processing. We store nothing.

Reconciliation requires us to temporarily retain your structured data (the text and numbers our AI extracts) so we can compare documents over time.

What we store: Structured text and numbers only (e.g., amounts, dates, vendor names, reference numbers).

What we never store: Your original PDFs, images, scans, or files.

How we protect it: AES-256 encryption at rest and in transit.

Your control: Delete all reconciliation data instantly from Settings — no support ticket required.

What we will never do: Use your data to train AI models, sell it, or share it with third parties.
